## Project Overview

Daily store sales at Rossmann fluctuate substantially due to promotional campaigns, competitive proximity, customer traffic patterns, and seasonal effects. Without accurate demand forecasting, retailers face costly overstocking or revenue-damaging stockouts.

This project builds a predictive model to forecast daily store-level sales using historical operational data, with a 12-week forward-looking forecast output.

**Hypotheses tested:**
- H1: Active promotions significantly increase daily store sales
- H2: Sales follow strong and predictable temporal patterns
- H3: Stores farther from competitors generate higher daily sales

All three hypotheses were supported by the data.

## Dataset

Source: Rossmann Store Sales Kaggle Competition

| Variable | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Target (Y) | Daily revenue generated by each store |
| Promo | Binary | Whether a standard promotion was active |
| SchoolHoliday | Binary | Indicator for school holiday |
| CompetitionDistance | Continuous | Distance (meters) to nearest competitor |

| Date | Date | Calendar date (engineered into Year, Month, DayOfWeek) |

**Scale:** ~1 million rows, 18 variables, ~1,000 stores across multiple years

## Methodology

### Data Cleaning
- Removed closed store days (Open = 0), which always have zero sales and distort model training
- Imputed missing CompetitionDistance values with the median
- Filtered zero-sales records on open store days

### Feature Engineering
- Extracted Year, Month, and DayOfWeek from the Date field
- Retained DayOfWeek as ordinal predictor given strong weekly patterns

### Train-Test Split
- 80% training, 20% held-out test set
- Note: random split used; time-ordered split would be more rigorous for time-series data

### Models
Two models were built and compared:

**Linear Regression** served as the baseline to establish a performance floor and provide directional interpretability for stakeholders.

**Random Forest** was the primary model. Selected because it handles right-skewed sales distributions naturally, captures non-linear relationships and implicit interactions (e.g., promotion on a Friday vs. Monday), and reduces overfitting through ensemble averaging.

### Evaluation Metrics
- RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)
- MAE (Mean Absolute Error)
- R-squared

---

## Model Results

| Model | RMSE (EUR) | MAE (EUR) | R-squared |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear Regression | 2,863.07 | 2,074.46 | 0.1500 |
| **Random Forest** | **2,604.00** | **1,891.73** | **0.2962** |

Random Forest outperformed Linear Regression across all three metrics:
- RMSE reduced by ~9%
- Average absolute error dropped by ~EUR 182 per store per day
- R-squared nearly doubled from 15% to 29.6%

**Known limitation:** Both models underestimate extreme high-sales days during strong promotional periods due to the rarity of those events and ensemble averaging smoothing effects.

## Key Findings

**Feature Importance (Random Forest ranking):**

| Rank | Feature | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Promo | Promotions are the single strongest driver of daily sales |
| 2 | CompetitionDistance | Stores farther from competitors perform better |
| 3 | DayOfWeek | Strong weekly demand variation, especially weekends |
| 4 | Month / Seasonality | December and holiday periods drive significant uplift |
| 5 | StoreType / Assortment | Secondary but meaningful effect on revenue |

Promotions influence sales approximately twice as strongly as competition distance.

**Promotional Effect:**
- Median daily sales increase 20-30% on promotional days
- Promotional days also show significantly higher variance, meaning promotions both raise average demand and increase the probability of extreme demand spikes
- This combination is the highest-risk forecasting scenario for operational planning

**Sales Distribution:**
- Most store-days fall between EUR 4,000 and EUR 10,000
- Distribution is right-skewed, with rare high-sales events during promotions
- Non-normality supports the use of tree-based methods over linear regression

**Temporal Patterns:**
- Day-of-week and month are consistent, predictable signals
- Pre-weekend days and December periods are high-demand windows requiring proactive inventory preparation

## Business Implications

**Inventory alignment:** Use model predictions to pre-position inventory ahead of promotional campaigns and high-demand periods, reducing both stockout and overstock risk.

**Staffing optimization:** Forecast-driven schedules reduce labor costs during low-demand periods while ensuring capacity during peaks.

**Promotion planning:** The 20-30% median sales uplift from promotions should be factored into promotional ROI calculations and supply chain planning.

**Competitive strategy:** Stores in highly competitive areas may benefit from differentiated promotion strategies to counteract proximity effects.

## Lessons Learned

**Preprocessing matters:** Closed store days must be excluded early in the pipeline. Keeping zero-sales records from closed stores significantly distorts model training.

**Build the baseline first:** Linear Regression's R-squared of 0.15 made Random Forest's 0.296 legible as a real improvement. Without the baseline, it's unclear whether 0.296 is good or disappointing.

**Use time-ordered splits for time-series data:** The random 80/20 split used here allows the model to learn from future data during training. A time-based split would produce more realistic out-of-sample performance estimates. Real-world performance would likely be lower than the test-set metrics suggest.

**Visualize first:** Plotting actual vs. predicted sales revealed the spike underestimation problem far more clearly than numeric metrics alone.
